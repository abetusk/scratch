Monty

 
 

This is a small experiment to help understand the Monty Hall problem. The Monty Hall problem is as follows:

 

You are given the choice of three doors. Behind one is a prize and behind the other two is nothing. You are allowed to initial pick one of the three doors. After your pick, another door opened and is shown to have nothing behind it. You are now given the option to stay with your initial pick or to switch doors. After you decide to stay or switch, the door you pick is finally revealed.

 
The question is: what is the best strategy for winning the prize?

Some people find the description ambiguous, so for clarities sake, here are some extra, explicit, conditions:
  • The door the prize is placed behind is chosen at random
  • After initial placement, the prize location never changes during the course of the game
  • After the initial pick, another door with nothing behind it, will always be revealed
  • You will always be given the chance to stay with your initial pick or switch to another door
  • Winning the prize is the desirable outcome
